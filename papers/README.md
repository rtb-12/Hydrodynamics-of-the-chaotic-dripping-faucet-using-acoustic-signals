# Source papers

Fourteen PDFs resolve to thirteen distinct works.
`0408096v1.pdf` is the arXiv preprint of the published JFM paper, so both are covered by one entry below.

The PDFs sit alongside this file.
Several are publisher-copyrighted, so the citations and DOIs below are the canonical reference for anyone wanting their own copy.

## Drop-impact acoustics

**Phillips, S., Agarwal, A., & Jordan, P. (2018).**
"The Sound Produced by a Dripping Tap is Driven by Resonant Oscillations of an Entrapped Air Bubble."
*Scientific Reports* **8**, 9515.
doi:[10.1038/s41598-018-27813-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-27813-0)
Open access, CC BY.
Establishes that the plink is the Minnaert oscillation of an entrained bubble, with two null experiments that silence the impact entirely.

**Chashechkin, Yu. D. (2024).**
"Acoustics and Hydrodynamics of the Drop Impact: Two Modes of Sound Packet Emission."
*Technical Physics* **69**(1), 85–94.
doi:[10.1134/S106378422470035X](https://doi.org/10.1134/S106378422470035X)
Intrusive and splash-formation modes, with discrete spectral components from 31 to 94 kHz.

**Vazquez, A., Manasseh, R., Sánchez, R. M., & Metcalfe, G. (2008).**
"Experimental comparison between acoustic and pressure signals from a bubbling flow."
*Chemical Engineering Science* **63**(24), 5860–5869.
doi:[10.1016/j.ces.2008.08.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ces.2008.08.032)
The only source applying nonlinear-dynamics tooling to an acoustic signal, on a submerged bubbler rather than a faucet.

**Pumphrey, H. C., & Walton, A. J. (1988).**
"Experimental study of the sound emitted by water drops impacting on a water surface."
*European Journal of Physics* **9**, 225–231.
The primary source for the entrainment window that Phillips later cites.
Names a **Brüel & Kjær 8103** hydrophone, and detects drops with a BDX 25 phototransistor into an NE 555 monostable.
Optimum fall height reported as 7–8 cm for 0.03 cm³ drops.

**Prokhorov, V. E., & Chashechkin, Yu. D. (2011).**
"Sound Generation as a Drop Falls on a Water Surface."
*Acoustical Physics* **57**(6), 807–818.
doi:[10.1134/S1063771011050137](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063771011050137)
Simultaneous air and underwater recording.
**GI-51B** hydrophone at 30 mV/Pa over 2 Hz–120 kHz, **MV-101** microphone at 1.58 V/Pa over 20 Hz–40 kHz.
Fall heights from 8 to 110 cm.

**Chashechkin, Yu. D., & Prokhorov, V. E. (2014).**
"Air and Underwater Sound Emission by Drop Impact on the Free Surface."
*Advances in Applied Acoustics* **3**.
doi:[10.14355/aiaas.2014.0301.01](https://doi.org/10.14355/aiaas.2014.0301.01)
The only paper in the collection publishing a complete sensor layout: microphone 22 cm above and 7 cm lateral, hydrophone 4 cm deep and 6 cm lateral.
Tank 145 × 50 × 60 cm.

**Chashechkin, Yu. D., & Prokhorov, V. E. (2023).**
"Acoustics of Periodic and Multiple Drop Impacts on a Water Surface."
*Acoustical Physics* **69**(3), 348–356.
doi:[10.1134/S1063771022700099](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063771022700099)
The closest paper to this project's configuration: a **periodic** drop train at T = 218 ms, roughly 4.6 drops per second.
Reports that shock pulses repeat stably from drop to drop while resonance packets do not, because the free surface has not settled between impacts.
Hydrophone centred on the drop line at 5 cm depth, chosen to reduce reverberation masking.

**Chashechkin, Yu. D., & Prokhorov, V. E. (2025).**
"Hydrodynamics and Acoustics of the Merging of a Drop with a Liquid in an Electrostatic Field."
*Fluid Dynamics* **60**:132.
doi:[10.1134/S0015462825603560](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0015462825603560)
**GI-54** hydrophone, 0.002–100 kHz. Basin 60 × 40 × 10 cm. Optical and acoustic synchronisation to 20 µs.

## Dripping-faucet chaos

**Sekatchev, M. (2023).**
"Chaotic Dynamics of a Dripping Water Faucet."
arXiv:[2307.05484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05484) [physics.flu-dyn]. University of British Columbia.
1.17 million drop timestamps from a passively draining tank; reports a 25 ms detector floor that caused missed drops.

**Roseberry, M. (2008).**
"Drip Drop: A Brief Study of the Dripping Faucet."
Unpublished student manuscript, Department of Physics, The College of Wooster.
Laser-and-photodiode gate into a National Instruments USB-6009 at 500 Hz.

**Ambravaneswaran, B., Phillips, S. D., & Basaran, O. A. (2000).**
"Theoretical Analysis of a Dripping Faucet."
*Physical Review Letters* **85**(25), 5332–5335.
First ab initio slender-jet theory; reports hysteresis, and finds no chaotic regime at Ohnesorge 0.1.

**Coullet, P., Mahadevan, L., & Riera, C. S. (2005).**
"Hydrodynamical models for the chaotic dripping faucet."
*Journal of Fluid Mechanics* **526**, 1–17.
doi:[10.1017/S0022112004002307](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112004002307)
Preprint: arXiv:[physics/0408096](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0408096).
Reduces dripping to a saddle-node bifurcation plus Shilnikov reinjection; embedding dimension 3 suffices.

**D'Innocenzo, A., & Renna, L. (1996).**
"Dripping Faucet."
*International Journal of Theoretical Physics* **35**(5), 941–973.
The variable-mass oscillator model used by the site's simulator, and the source of the 2200-drop chaotic transients that set the minimum run length.
