Drop intervals, whole recording

Both plots show the time gap between one drop and the next. Interval per drop is the plain view: gap number across, gap length up. A flat line is steady dripping, a line that climbs or falls is a drip that is slowing or speeding up, and a line that flips between two levels is period doubling. The return map plots each gap against the gap that follows it. Identical gaps pile onto one spot on the dashed diagonal. Points marching along the diagonal are a slow drift. A curve or folded band, with gaps jumping up and down, is what chaos would look like.

Sound of each drop

For each drop the microphone heard, the pitch is the strongest tone at the loudest moment of its sound. A lower pitch means a bigger thing is ringing, like a bigger bell. The pitch return map asks whether one drop's pitch predicts the next: points on the diagonal mean it repeats, a shapeless cloud means it does not. Pitch per drop shows the pitches in order, with a hollow mark for each drop that landed silently. Pitch against delay shows how long after the drop its sound came; separate clumps are separate ways of making a sound. A pale dot is a smeared sound whose pitch is uncertain.
