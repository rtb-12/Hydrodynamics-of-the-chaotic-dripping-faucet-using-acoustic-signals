The lab

Five instruments. The first two run the actual mass-spring faucet model from D’Innocenzo and Renna 1996, integrated live in your browser. The third lets you hear what a bubble of a given size sounds like. The last two are design calculators for the rig.

Turn the sound on

The simulator synthesises a plink for every drop, at the Minnaert frequency of the bubble that drop would entrain. Push the flow rate up and you can hear the rhythm split, and split again, and come apart. That is the whole thesis of this project, audible.

Regime is read from the last 48 intervals by looking for the smallest period p with |Tn − Tn+p| below 0.2 ms throughout. Changing R clears the history, because a different flow rate is a different attractor.

The model is Eq. 7 of D’Innocenzo and Renna with their breakoff law (i), at their Table I parameters: k = 475 dyn/cm, b = 1 g/s, xc = 0.19 cm, α = 0.25 cm−1s. Nothing is tuned for appearance. The drop detaches when the centre of mass falls past xc, sheds a mass proportional to its momentum, and the residue keeps oscillating, which is exactly the memory that makes the system chaotic.

This is the figure the whole field is built around, generated from the same integrator running above. Compare it with Sekatchev’s measured version: his real faucet did not climb the ladder cleanly, returning from period-2 to period-1 and only becoming chaotic past 34 ml/min. Model and reality disagree, and that disagreement is a large part of what makes this worth measuring properly.

Level-drift check

The argument for putting an overflow on the lower tank, in numbers you can move. Without one the impact tank fills, the water surface climbs toward the nozzle, and the impact velocity falls during the run.

Free fall ignores air drag, which is negligible at these heights: at 86 mm it gives 1.299 m/s against the 1.29 m/s Phillips report using the full terminal-velocity correction.

Verified against the paper This integrator gives period-1 to period-2 between R = 0.605 and 0.610. D’Innocenzo report 0.61. Intervals span 0.026 to 0.076 s against their 0.025 to 0.078 s.
Playback speed Real intervals here are 25 to 80 ms, so unslowed the faucet buzzes rather than drips. 0.2× puts it at a recognisable rate.
Pitch mapping is illustrative Drop volume gives drop diameter; bubble diameter is taken as 0.1775 of it, extrapolated from Phillips’ single measured pair of 4.0 mm and 0.71 mm. One data point is not a law.
