Choosing the sensors

Two channels in two media: a microphone in air and a hydrophone in water. The single decision that matters more than any model number is that both must record a waveform, not a detection.

Why a waveform, and not a sound detector

A detector tells you that something happened. A waveform tells you what happened. For an experiment whose entire question is what the sound can reveal, that difference decides whether the project works.

The same five drops, seen two ways

period-2 dripping

Three reasons, most important first

1. You cannot check a detector’s work. Your first real result is proving the microphone does not miss drops. Run 3000 drops: the optical gate counts 2851, the microphone counts 2847. Four missing. Why?

With a detector that is the end of the story, because the evidence no longer exists. With a waveform you go to those four moments and look. Genuinely silent, so no bubble formed? A plink just under threshold? A door slamming? Each answer leads to a different fix. A detector gives you a number; a waveform gives you evidence.

2. A detector forces you to guess before you know anything. The threshold is set by a trimpot, turned before the experiment, based on a guess about how loud a plink will be. Guess wrong and every run is wrong, and you find out afterwards. With a recording the threshold is a software decision made after the fact, so you can try five of them on the same audio and change your mind in month six about data from month one.

Record the raw thing, decide later

A detector bakes an irreversible decision into hardware at the moment of capture. A recording keeps the decision separate from the data. Over a year you will get a threshold wrong; the only question is whether that costs an afternoon of reprocessing or a month of re-recording.

3. The pitch is your second measurement. Big drop, low note. Small drop, high note. Drop size varies with flow rate, so the note carries information about the system’s state. That second channel is the part of this project nobody has done, and a detector discards it at the sensor before it ever reaches the computer.

In plain terms: a sound detector is a doorbell, which rings when someone arrives. A waveform is a recording of the conversation, which tells you who arrived, what they said, and still remembers when you later wonder whether anyone came at three o’clock.

How to tell whether a module gives you a waveform

Cheap "sound sensor" modules are everywhere and most of them are detectors. Three checks, strongest first.

CheckDetectorRecorder
The chip markingLM393 — a dual comparator with an open-collector output. Physically cannot produce an analogue signalLM358, LM386, MAX4466 — op-amps, which amplify
Pin count3 pins: VCC, GND, OUT4 pins, one marked AO or A0
The listing wording"switch value", "output low level when there has voice""analog output", "audio output"
The definitive testWire OUT into an oscilloscope or a laptop audio input and clap. A clean square step means digital. A messy wiggle that looks like sound means analogue

The nuance worth knowing: a 4-pin version of the same board would give a waveform on its analogue pin. But at that point you have paid for an electret capsule plus a comparator you are ignoring plus two LEDs you do not want. The useful part of those boards is the microphone, and a bare capsule costs a third as much. Put the difference into the interface, which is where sample rate and channel count actually live.

Note Pumphrey’s: 50 Hz to 14 kHz, an ordinary dynamic microphone. He still resolved bubble tones around 7 kHz with it. The airborne channel does not need an exotic transducer, it needs adequate response at the bubble frequency and an honest recording chain.

Where to put them

The AIAAS 2014 paper is the only one in the collection that publishes a complete sensor layout, in a single figure caption:

The 2023 paper adds the reason for centring the hydrophone rather than offsetting it:

"The hydrophone was placed in the center of the tank at a depth of 5 cm, on the line of incidence of a single drop… This arrangement made it possible to reduce the masking effect of reverberation associated with scattering from the bottom and walls of the tank."

Directly below the impact maximises the direct path’s advantage over every reflected one. Vazquez’s 5 cm lateral standoff was to avoid perturbing a submerged bubbling nozzle, which is a different geometry from drop impact. For your rig, centre it.

What to buy

The microphone barely matters. The recorder is what matters, because it sets your sample rate and how many channels share one clock.

Why four channels is non-negotiable: microphone, hydrophone, optical gate and one spare must share a single converter clock. A two-channel interface cannot run the Phase 1 cross-validation at all, and that cross-validation is the project’s first result. See the signal chain.

Precedent for going cheap on the transducers: Vazquez extracted correlation dimensions and chaos transitions using a Sound Blaster sound card at 44.1 kHz, and Pumphrey resolved 7 kHz bubble tones with a 14 kHz dynamic microphone. Consumer-grade capture is proven adequate for this class of measurement.

Order of purchase

Phone test this week. If plinks come out cleanly, buy the interface. The electret and the piezo are pocket change beside it, and the proper hydrophone can wait until Phase 2 has a reason to exist.

Two media, two sensors A microphone cannot work underwater. Water’s acoustic impedance is about 3600 times air’s, so a diaphragm tuned for air barely moves. Hydrophones use piezoelectric ceramic instead.
Signal strength Phillips measured ~40 Pa underwater against ~0.3 Pa airborne, about 40 dB. The hydrophone is the easier channel; the microphone is the one that could be deployed.
Preamp at the element A piezo is a high-impedance capacitive source. Cable capacitance forms a divider with it and eats exactly the high frequencies you care about.
