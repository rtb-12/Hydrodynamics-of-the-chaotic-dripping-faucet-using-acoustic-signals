Dripping Faucet Acoustics B.Tech project, 2026–27

Drop sound and fall height

The same faucet at the same flow rate, about one drop a second; only the height the drops fall into the water changes. Each row is one phone recording, its waveform and spectrogram scrolling with its own sound. Play a row, click a graph to move there, or step from drop to drop; each row starts on a typical drop.

The graphs follow the selected audio. Cleaned has the music and hum below 1 kHz filtered out and the steady hiss turned down; the drops themselves are untouched. Every row is drawn on one scale, so a fainter drop looks fainter. Dashed lines mark drops, coloured where the drop rings. Above 9 kHz the phone’s compression makes up the sound, so ignore that part of the spectrogram.

What changes with height

A drop rings when it traps a small air bubble that keeps sounding a clear tone for a few milliseconds; without one it only clicks. In the plots each dot is one drop, spread a little sideways around its height so dots do not hide each other, and a bar marks the median. Faint dots are drops that did not ring. ρ is the rank correlation with height: near ±1 is a steady trend, near 0 none.
