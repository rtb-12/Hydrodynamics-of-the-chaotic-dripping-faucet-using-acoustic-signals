Four ways to hold a flow rate

Flow rate is the bifurcation parameter, so how each group controlled it matters more than almost any other choice they made. The literature contains three answers, and this project proposes a fourth.

MethodUsed byStabilityCost of the choice
Syringe pumpAmbravaneswaran 2000±1%, statedExpensive, and the barrel empties, so run length is bounded
Constant-pressure chamberVazquez 2008not quantifiedWorks for gas. The liquid equivalent is a constant-head tank
Metering valve on a bottleRoseberry 2008Head falls as the bottle drainsRuns became non-repeatable. The author says so explicitly
Passive head decaySekatchev 2023Drifts by designNeeds per-block detrending. No operating point can be revisited
Constant-head overflow weirThis projectPinned by geometry, not by a pumpNeeds a sump and a pump. Degenerates into a passive sweep on demand

What none of them did

Three rigs listen. Three rigs count drops. Not one does both. Phillips and Chashechkin built excellent acoustic instruments and pointed them at isolated single drops, so no interval sequence exists in their data. Sekatchev and Roseberry built interval recorders and never attached a microphone. Vazquez did acoustic chaos properly, but on a submerged bubbler rather than a faucet.

Hysteresis warning Ambravaneswaran et al. report hysteresis, so an increasing flow ramp will not match a decreasing one. Record ramp direction with every run.
